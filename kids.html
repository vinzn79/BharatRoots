7.1

Picture stories: Ramayana and Mahabharata

Read along one page at a time. Tap Next to turn the page. (Animated versions are coming soon.)

For grown-ups: These retellings are short, gentle and free of frightening detail. Pause to ask, "How do you think they felt?" There are many versions of these epics across India and Southeast Asia, including Valmiki's, Kamban's in Tamil and Tulsidas's in Awadhi. It's fine if your family tells them differently.

Festival crafts

Paper kite (Sankranti)

Cut a diamond from coloured paper, glue two thin sticks in a cross, and add a ribbon tail.

Handmade rakhi

Braid three coloured threads, then glue a paper flower or big bead in the middle.

Clay diya (Diwali)

Pinch a ball of air-dry clay into a little bowl with a spout. Paint it when dry. Grown-ups can add a battery tea-light.

Paper-petal pookalam (Onam)

Cut petals from coloured paper and arrange them in rings on a paper plate.

Eid lantern card

Fold card into a lantern shape, cut star-shaped windows, and back them with yellow tissue paper.

Christmas star (Kerala style)

Make a five-pointed paper star lantern from coloured card and decorate it with Warli figures.

7.5

Grandparent storytime coming soon

A growing audio library of grandparents, parents and community elders telling folk tales, festival memories and childhood stories in their own languages: Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Bengali, Gujarati, Malayalam, Marathi, Kannada, Punjabi, Urdu, Odia and more.

Start your own today: Ask a grandparent to tell a 3–5 minute story. Record it on a phone, write down the title and language, and save it as a family treasure. When submissions open, families will be able to share their recordings here, with permission.

Learning Resources and Source Links

We link major claims to credible sources so you can check them and go deeper. These are the places we recommend most.

Official cultural and heritage sources

Indian Culture Portal

Government of India's digital library of heritage: festivals, arts, monuments, rare books, archives and maps.

Ministry of Culture

Policies, institutions, intangible heritage and cultural schemes.

Archaeological Survey of India

Protected monuments, excavations, visiting information and photography rules.

Know India

Government overview of states, national symbols, history and culture.

Incredible India

Ministry of Tourism's official guide to destinations, festivals and experiences.

National Portal of India

Official information, including the government holiday calendar and state links.

World heritage

UNESCO World Heritage Centre: India

Every World Heritage Site in India, with official descriptions.

UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage: India

Living traditions recognised by UNESCO, including Vedic chanting, Ramlila, Kumbh Mela, Yoga, Durga Puja in Kolkata, Garba and Deepavali.

Museums, archives and primary sources

National Museum, New Delhi

Collections from Harappan seals to Chola bronzes and miniature paintings.

Abhilekh Patal (National Archives of India)

Digitised historical records, including freedom-movement documents.

The Constitution of India

The official text from the Legislative Department.

National Mission for Manuscripts

India's effort to document and preserve millions of manuscripts.

Arts, education and maps

Sangeet Natak Akademi

India's national academy of music, dance and drama.

Centre for Cultural Resources and Training

Teacher resources on Indian art and culture.

NCERT textbooks

Free school textbooks on history, geography and civics, a good base for students.

National Digital Library of India

Millions of free books, papers and learning materials.

Survey of India

India's official mapping agency.

MyGov

Citizen portal with cultural campaigns and national-day resources.

Our editorial standard

  • Major historical and factual claims link to an official, museum, archival or peer-reviewed source.
  • Where historians disagree, we say so and present the main views.
  • Where evidence is missing (for example, the Indus script), we say "we don't know yet."
  • Religious and community traditions are described as their practitioners understand them, respectfully and without ranking them.
  • Festival dates are given as typical months; check current calendars for exact dates.
  • We correct errors promptly. Readers are encouraged to send corrections with sources.

India's Ministry of Culture describes its mission as preserving and promoting all forms of art and culture, tangible and intangible. That is the spirit of this hub too.

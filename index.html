It is a living civilization shaped by thousands of years of history, many languages, regional customs, spiritual traditions, artistic practices, and family memories. This page brings together beginner-friendly lessons that help diaspora families and curious learners explore India with curiosity, respect, and context.

India in 10 Minutes

A quick tour to get your bearings before you dive into any topic.

The land

India is the seventh-largest country by area. The Himalayas rise in the north, the wide Indo-Gangetic plains spread below them, the Thar Desert lies to the west and the Deccan plateau fills the south. Long coastlines run along the Arabian Sea, the Indian Ocean and the Bay of Bengal. The monsoon rains, arriving roughly from June to September, shape farming, festivals and daily life.

The people

India is the world's most populous country, home to more than 1.4 billion people. It has 28 states and 8 union territories. The Constitution lists 22 scheduled languages, and hundreds more are spoken at home. Hindi and English are used for central government work, while each state has its own official language or languages.

The story

One of the world's earliest cities rose in the Indus Valley about 4,500 years ago. Then came the Vedic period, the Mauryan and Gupta empires, powerful southern kingdoms like the Cholas, the Delhi Sultanate and the Mughals, the Marathas and other regional powers, and nearly two centuries of British rule. India became independent on 15 August 1947 and a republic on 26 January 1950.

The faiths

Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism and Sikhism began in India. Islam, Christianity, Zoroastrianism and Judaism have been part of Indian life for many centuries. Many tribal and folk traditions also flourish. Festivals from all of these traditions fill the calendar.

The table

Indian food changes every few hundred kilometres. You'll find wheat breads and dairy in the north, rice, lentils and coconut in the south, fish and mustard in the east, and sweet-savoury vegetarian thalis in the west. Vegetarian cooking is especially rich, and Jain cooking avoids root vegetables too.

The arts

India has two great classical music systems, Hindustani in the north and Carnatic in the south. It has several classical dance forms, such as Bharatanatyam and Kathak, and folk and tribal arts such as Warli and Madhubani. Its film industries are among the busiest in the world.
